CONTRIBUTE

Help challenge and improve Open Conduct.

Tell us the idea is wrong. Tell us which part. Point at a person or a situation the work has misread, or show a better way to do it. You do not have to agree with any of this to be useful here.

CURRENT STATUS

Submissions are reviewed in batches. The project may not reply to every submission individually.

WHAT HELPS

Send one thing. Make it the part you would argue with.

  • A factual error, or an explanation that confused you
  • A consumer, worker, cultural, disability, or international perspective
  • A use case, edge case, misuse case, or failure mode
  • A legal, labor, competition, security, privacy, or measurement concern
  • A relevant study, standard, case, or historical comparison
  • A better question, calculation, interface, architecture, or protection
  • An implementation constraint from a real workflow
  • A response to a published open question
  • An introduction to someone the project should learn from
  • Interest in nonexclusive research or implementation discovery

DO NOT SUBMIT

Please keep these to yourself.

  • Real customer conduct histories or scores
  • Worker responses or information that could identify an individual worker
  • Identity documents, credentials, authentication secrets, or security details
  • Confidential company, client, employee, or personal information
  • Information about another person
  • Material you do not have the right to share
  • Substantial code, specification rewrites, datasets, or patent-sensitive methods before contributor terms are published
  • A request to change or dispute someone's score, since no scores exist yet

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

What happens to what you send.

01

Reviewed in batches

Everything gets sorted by what it touches, then read together rather than one at a time.

02

It might change something

Your point could become a correction, a clearer explanation, a new open question, or a documented objection. It could also change nothing, and that gets written down too.

03

Attributed only with permission

The project may summarize recurring feedback without identifying people. A name or quotation is not used as endorsement without separate permission.

HOW THE PROPOSAL CHANGES

Not every change is the same size.

Editorial correction

Fixes presentation without changing meaning.

Clarification

Makes an existing decision easier to understand.

Candidate revision

Changes a provisional construct, method, interface, or research direction.

Structural amendment

Changes a core protection, purpose, prohibition, or frozen architecture and therefore requires a new release identity and impact review.

FOUNDER-STEWARDED STAGE

Brent decides what gets published. That cannot outlast the proposal stage.

Brent Turner is the visible final steward of publication decisions during the proposal stage. He may correct, clarify, add open questions, publish alternatives, and advance candidate research.

He may not weaken a constitutional protection, whether quietly or in the open, and may not present a founder decision as multi-stakeholder consensus. Independent rights and decision structures become mandatory before any live system affects people.
