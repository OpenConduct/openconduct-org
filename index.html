OPEN DRAFT STANDARD

The open standard behind the Conduct Quotient.

Businesses can tell you what a customer spends. None of them can tell you how that customer treats the people who serve them. Open Conduct is a draft standard for a private score that answers the second question, built from protected employee responses and approved business records, held by the customer rather than the company. Ordinary service and legal rights would never depend on it. This site is where the design gets argued with.

HOW A CONDUCT QUOTIENT IS FORMED AND USED

01 · HUMAN EXPERIENCE

Net Conduct Score (NCS)

Eligible employees answer one protected question about the interaction.

02 · APPROVED RECORDS

Verified Conduct Index (VCI)

Approved records cover only eligible responsibilities within the customer's control.

03 · ONE CONTEXTUAL SCORE

Conduct Quotient (CQ)

When both inputs qualify, they form one private score for one named context.

04 · CUSTOMER-REQUESTED USE

One answer for the business

The business learns only whether the customer qualifies for one approved benefit.

CURRENT STATUS

Open Conduct is a public proposal, not a live system.

The standard is still being developed and tested. There is no Conduct Quotient anyone can receive today, and no consumer network, certified implementation, alliance, partner program, benefit, or report product is operating.

CURRENT DESIGN RULES

Four rules the design will not bend on.

Govern the two inputs separately

NCS records eligible human experience. VCI uses approved operational evidence. Each must clear its own gates, and strength in one cannot erase a material problem in the other.

Lead with one contextual Conduct Quotient

When both inputs qualify, CQ presents one private score from 1.00 to 5.00. The two inputs remain available beneath it for explanation, correction, and challenge.

Name the scope and prove every transfer

A company CQ, an industry CQ, and an overall CQ are different claims. Any transfer needs an approved source, destination, and purpose that explain why the history is relevant.

Share the smallest useful result

The consumer starts the request. A receiving business ordinarily learns only whether the customer qualifies for one approved benefit.

BENEFIT DELIVERY

Conduct decides who qualifies. Something else hands out the reward.

A Conduct implementation should produce the minimum approved result. A separate, replaceable system can turn that result into a real benefit through the business's existing channels.

Conduct governs eligibility

The Conduct layer determines whether a customer qualifies for one named, additional benefit under one approved policy. It governs the score, evidence, consent, proof, protections, correction, and challenge rules.

An incentive platform can deliver the benefit

Loyalty and promotion infrastructure can apply points, a perk, an upgrade, or another approved reward after receiving the limited result. Talon.One is one example of this partner category; no partnership or endorsement exists today.

The boundary must remain narrow

The delivery platform ordinarily receives only the eligibility result, purpose, policy version, and expiry. It does not receive CQ, NCS, VCI, source companies, employee responses, or underlying events.

No provider becomes required infrastructure

Conduct should work with multiple incentive, loyalty, commerce, and customer-experience systems. A delivery provider cannot recalculate CQ, redefine conduct, turn eligibility into a reusable audience, or make ordinary service depend on the result.
