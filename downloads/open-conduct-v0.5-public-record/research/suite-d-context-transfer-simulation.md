# Conduct Ecosystem — Suite D Context Transfer Simulation Report v0.1

## The answer in human terms

The defining portability idea survives—but not as a score that travels everywhere.

The strongest transfer candidate is evidence from an independent business where the consumer has the same role and the interaction serves a closely matched function. That is the responsible version of the Delta-to-United example. It does not mean that United receives a Delta score or history. It means an approved airline-purpose proof may use eligible history from a genuinely comparable airline setting without telling United which competitor supplied it.

The simulation also found something strategically useful: **matched interaction function matters more than a broad industry label**. Matched work across adjacent industries often preserved more signal than different work inside the same industry. “Travel,” “hospitality,” or “retail” is not a sufficiently precise context map.

The resulting architecture is:

1. Every source-to-target transfer begins at zero.
2. NCS and VCI transfer separately because they measure different things.
3. Each mapping is approved for one evidence class, interaction function, consumer role, proof purpose, population, jurisdiction, channel, and period.
4. A company-specific relationship is useful local evidence but not proof of portability.
5. Shared platforms, databases, or corporate ownership do not count as independent validation.
6. The verifier receives only the approved purpose-bound result—not the source company, source count, event volume, or history composition.
7. A mapping can return to zero when performance, fairness, meaning, independence, law, or comprehension changes.

This narrows portability enough to make it more credible without removing the magic of the original idea.

## 1. What was tested

Suite D compared:

| ID | Context family | Role in the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| D0 | No transfer | Required zero-transfer null |
| D1 | Same business, same role and function | Strong local-reference case; not cross-company portability |
| D2 | Independent business, closely matched role and function | Lead cross-company research candidate |
| D3 | Same industry, different role or function | Stress test of industry-label transfer |
| D4 | Adjacent industry, matched interaction function | Test of functional relevance across industry boundaries |
| D5 | Unrelated or materially different context | Presumptive zero-transfer case |
| D6 | Shared platform or corporate family presented as independent | False-independence stress case |

The full run included:

- 7 Suite D scenario-and-mode groups
- 357 unique common synthetic histories
- 1,200 synthetic consumers per history
- 8 target sites per history
- 2,040 candidate-case evaluations
- Human-interaction and operational-reliability transfer evaluated separately
- Negative through strong declared source-target relevance
- Population shift, site heterogeneity, group error, shared infrastructure, source inference, small-cell reconstruction, and repeated-query attacks
- Identical generated history for every candidate within each comparison

The preserved result package is `simulation/run_packages/suite-d-v0.1-results.json`.

## 2. Closely matched independent contexts are the lead portability candidate

Across the entire deliberately hostile test space, D2's median human-dimension increment over zero was modest at 0.018 and its median site replication rate was 50%. That broad average includes negative source-target relevance, severe population shift, and high site heterogeneity; it is not the correct basis for approving transfer.

In the subset where the test declared high source-target relevance with bounded population shift and site heterogeneity, D2 produced:

- Median human-dimension increment over zero: **0.427**
- Median replication across target sites: **100%**
- Median precision for a favorable target outcome: **0.585**
- Median protected-group accuracy gap: **0.013**

Those figures show structural possibility under favorable assumptions. They do not estimate Delta, United, airlines, or any real population. The relevance was deliberately supplied by the generator so the simulation could test whether the architecture preserves or destroys it. A real mapping still has to establish that relevance with independent field evidence.

D2 becomes the lead cross-company **research family**, not an approved production map.

## 3. Same industry is not enough

D3 tested whether a shared industry label could substitute for matched work. It could not.

Across the full stress space, D3's median human-dimension increment was slightly negative at −0.003, with only 12.5% of sites exceeding the modest replication marker. Even in the favorable relevance subset, median increment was only 0.026 and median proof precision was 0.328.

This does not prove that every same-industry, different-function mapping is invalid. It proves that **industry membership cannot authorize the mapping**. A particular role-and-function pairing may still earn nonzero relevance, but it must do so as a specifically defined mapping rather than inheriting relevance from the word “airline,” “hotel,” or “restaurant.”

## 4. Interaction function can cross industry boundaries

D4 tested a matched interaction function in an adjacent industry. Its performance was consistently stronger than the same-industry, different-function case:

- D4 exceeded D3 in **78.4%** of paired human-dimension cases.
- In the favorable relevance subset, D4's median increment was **0.282**.
- Median target-site replication in that subset was **100%**.
- Median proof precision was **0.510**.

The synthetic coefficients encode the hypothesis that function matters, so these numbers cannot prove a real adjacent-industry mapping. They do establish the correct research taxonomy: context mapping should begin with the actual human and operational function, not an industry directory.

For example, a governed customer-support interaction may ultimately have more in common across an airline and a hotel than it does with boarding behavior and baggage responsibility inside one airline. That mapping would still need separate NCS and VCI evidence, population and channel tests, comprehension, and legal review.

## 5. Company-specific fit is not portability

D1 was the strongest favorable synthetic case, as expected. In the high-relevance, bounded-shift subset, its median increment was 0.606 with replication across every target site.

That is useful, but it answers a different question. A company learning that its own established customer relationship is meaningful is not evidence that the history travels to another company. D1 is the local benchmark that a portable mapping should be compared against; it cannot be described as cross-company validation.

This distinction protects the claims ladder:

- A company-specific CQ state may recognize an established local relationship.
- An industry-purpose proof requires at least one independently validated cross-company map.
- A broader portability claim requires materially different replication.

## 6. Unrelated contexts remain at zero

D5 produced essentially no true incremental value. Its full-space median was approximately zero, and its favorable-subset proof precision remained close to the 25% base rate.

Unrelated or materially different settings therefore remain presumptively zero. More data, a strong source score, consumer consent, or a commercial desire for a result does not create relevance.

Zero is not a punishment or an adverse score. It means the target proof cannot responsibly use that source context.

## 7. Shared infrastructure can manufacture apparent portability

D6 deliberately allowed source and target records to share an administrative artifact. Across all D6 cases, apparent increment exceeded the independence-adjusted increment by a median of 0.015. In 23.0% of cases, the inflation exceeded 0.05; at the extreme, inflation reached 0.244.

This is a major partnership and evaluation warning. Two brands can appear to corroborate each other because they use:

- The same marketplace or customer-support platform
- A shared corporate parent
- The same identity, fraud, CRM, or reservation database
- The same policy, rater pool, implementation team, or derived feature
- One issuer's record repackaged by another operator

The system must model administrative and evidentiary dependence before it calls evidence cross-company. A platform relationship can make implementation easier; it cannot, by itself, establish independent portability.

## 8. NCS and VCI need separate mappings

The simulation evaluated human-interaction and operational-reliability transfer independently. A mapping that preserves the meaning of employee experience does not automatically preserve the meaning of operational responsibilities, and vice versa.

Each production mapping therefore needs separate authorization for:

- NCS response meaning, rater population, sampling, role, language, culture, and interaction channel
- VCI responsibility class, notice, consumer control, evidence source, business-side context, attribution, and lifecycle state
- The exact proof purpose that is allowed to use either or both dimensions

One dimension may remain at zero while the other advances. If a proof requires both, both mappings and both component gates must clear independently.

## 9. The Delta-to-United example remains—but becomes precise

The canonical example can remain:

> A consumer may choose to use an approved airline-purpose CQ proof at United that is supported by eligible, relevant history earned elsewhere, including at Delta.

The ordinary protocol does **not** tell United:

- That Delta was a source
- How many companies or interactions contributed
- The consumer's Delta NCS, VCI, CQ, events, disputes, or relationship length
- Whether one source dominated the result
- What the result would have been under another purpose or source combination

The consumer privately authorizes an already approved context-and-purpose proof. The source set is fixed before the request, the verifier cannot shop across competitors or purposes, and equivalent queries are metered and audited.

## 10. Privacy has to be part of transfer—not added later

The minimized proof path produced no named-source disclosure in the structural model and held source inference to the modeled chance baseline. That result depends on strong protocol rules: fixed source composition, a single purpose-bound result, cell suppression, and equivalent-query controls.

The counterfactual design allowed the verifier to vary source or purpose composition and repeat queries. Median modeled source-inference accuracy rose from the 25% chance baseline to 40.5%, with a 90th percentile of 54.5% and a maximum of 81.1%. Repeated small-cell queries produced reconstruction risk as high as 60.8% in the stress model.

These are structural risk indicators, not forecasts. They show why consumer consent alone is inadequate. A verifier must not receive an open-ended “check this person's score across these sources” interface.

## 11. The mapping registry becomes a core system object

Every nonzero transfer should exist as a public, versioned mapping profile containing at least:

- Source and target context
- NCS or VCI evidence class
- Consumer role and interaction function
- Proof purpose and approved benefit class
- Population, language, jurisdiction, channel, labor model, and relevant power relationship
- Eligible issuers and independence rules
- Training, validation, replication, and monitoring periods
- Incremental validity, calibration, error, subgroup, concentration, and comprehension evidence
- Legal and governance approvals
- Effective date, expiry, version, owner, challenge path, and rollback trigger

The output is not “airline transfers at 0.7.” It is a bounded authorization such as “this evidence class from this interaction function may support this proof purpose for this population and period, subject to these gates.”

## 12. Candidate verdicts

| Candidate | Suite D verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| D0: No transfer | **Retain as permanent default and null** | Every unapproved mapping remains zero |
| D1: Same business, same function | **Retain as local reference** | Useful company-specific evidence, but not portability |
| D2: Independent, closely matched function | **Lead portability research family** | Strongest independent cross-company performance under favorable declared conditions |
| D3: Same industry, different function | **Do not advance by industry label** | Broad industry membership did not preserve sufficient meaning |
| D4: Adjacent industry, matched function | **Retain as bounded research family** | Functional match may outperform industry match, but each map must earn approval |
| D5: Unrelated context | **Presumptive zero** | No meaningful incremental value; consent and data volume cannot create relevance |
| D6: Shared infrastructure | **Fail independence as presented** | Administrative dependence can manufacture apparent validation |

No candidate is approved for a real-person proof, benefit, or production release.

## 13. What remains unresolved

Suite D cannot establish:

- Whether Delta and United, or any real company pair, share a valid mapping
- The correct taxonomy of interaction functions, roles, responsibilities, channels, or purposes
- Minimum incremental value, calibration, replication, fairness, comprehension, or monitoring thresholds
- Whether consumers understand why one history transfers and another does not
- Whether a mapping is proportionate and lawful for a specific benefit and jurisdiction
- How a real protocol performs against auxiliary-data, collusion, composition, and repeated-query attacks
- How much independent issuer diversity is required for an industry proof
- Whether any cross-industry or overall CQ should ever exist

These questions require predeclared shadow studies, materially independent replication, consumer and worker research, privacy red teaming, legal review, and a bounded benefit design.

## 14. Decision and next step

Portability should advance as a **registry of exact, revocable mappings**, not a property inherited by a score.

The United–Delta proposition remains the lead illustration of the future value. But the system earns it one mapping at a time: same purpose, closely matched function, separately valid NCS and VCI evidence, independent sources, bounded populations, minimized proof, and no named-competitor disclosure.

Suite D therefore preserves the ambition while rejecting universal reputation architecture. The next structural comparison is Suite E: whether that portable positive proof and its benefit can remain genuinely voluntary and upside-only—or whether nonpresentation becomes a shadow penalty.
