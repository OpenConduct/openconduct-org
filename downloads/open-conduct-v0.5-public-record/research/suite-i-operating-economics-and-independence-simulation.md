## 1. Executive result

The economic model is viable only as a staged model.

The strongest Stage 3 direction combines **I2 diversified pilot funding** with **I3 a ring-fenced fiscal sponsor or hosted administrative home**. These are complementary, not mutually exclusive: I2 describes who pays and how funding is diversified; I3 describes where the project can hold funds, contracts, providers, and controls without asking Brent to build a complete institution before the pilot is ready.

The combination advances only if the host cannot control standards, evidence, appeals, evaluation, findings, publication, or exit; all required pilot and tail funds are committed before launch; every in-kind dependency is valued at replacement cost; and new intake pauses before rights capacity is exhausted.

The other candidates resolve as follows:

- **I1 single anchor sponsorship** can close a small ordinary pilot, but does not become the lead because practical control, revocability, and simultaneous loss of cash and in-kind support remain too concentrated.
- **I4 early independent OCA** is a valid later alternative when the added legal, financial, administrative, governance, insurance, and operating burden is itself fully funded. Incorporation does not create independence when one sponsor, vendor, founder, or platform still controls continuation.
- **I5 the diversified Stage 4 mix** becomes the production destination. It is not launch financing and cannot be imported backward as if membership, certification, services, and reports already have demonstrated demand.
- **I6 report-funded launch** is rejected. Even when modeled report revenue eventually closes its ordinary operating gap, protected public goods and tail obligations remain materially unfunded.

The central financial rule is now simple:

> Stage 3 is a prepaid, capped, complete research-and-operation program. Stage 4 is where diversified recurring economics must prove themselves.

This suite does not produce a dollar budget. It establishes what the future bottom-up budget must include and which funding structures remain eligible to fund it.

## 2. What this means in human terms

The idea does not need to become Brent's second job, form a global alliance immediately, or sell reports before the network exists.

The practical path is:

1. Keep Stage 0 small, public, data-free, asynchronous, and easy to pause.
2. Accept funded Stage 1 and Stage 2 work only in bounded scopes.
3. When a credible pilot coalition exists, place the money and contracts in a competent host or fiscal sponsor unless a fully funded independent entity is then clearly better.
4. Require several funding sources or equivalent counterweights rather than letting the first major platform become the practical owner.
5. Pre-fund the complete pilot, the difficult cases, and the post-pilot tail before the first real person is affected.
6. Build recurring membership, certification, service, training, grant, and report revenue only after the system has earned the right to enter production.

That makes the economics serve the original vision. It does not require a pivot into a centralized infrastructure company, data broker, certification mill, or founder-operated support organization.

## 3. Question tested

Suite I tested whether the project can:

- Fund every promised consumer and worker right at the capped pilot volume
- Fund accessibility, evaluation, public-interest reporting, incident response, remedy, and wind-down before exposure
- Remain viable with Stage 3 report revenue set to zero
- Survive withdrawal by a dominant cash or in-kind supporter
- Prevent funding from controlling evidence, standards, publication, certification, appeal, or enforcement
- Pause new exposure before queues and tail obligations exceed funded capacity
- Avoid certification, appeal, insurance, service, and pricing incentives that undermine the system
- Protect Brent from an unfunded always-on Stage 0 role
- Reach a diversified Stage 4 without monetizing personal conduct data

The simulation does not ask whether a particular sponsor will fund the work or what the work will cost. Those remain real-world Stage 2 questions.

## 4. Candidates

| Candidate | Structure | Intended role |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | Single anchor-sponsored capped pilot | Early-pilot funding with protected common funds and participant-specific budgets |
| I2 | Multi-partner assessment plus unrestricted grant | Diversified Stage 3 funding |
| I3 | Fiscal sponsor or hosted project | Administrative and contractual home through Stage 3 |
| I4 | Early independent OCA entity | Separate institution with contracted operators and full overhead |
| I5 | Stage 4 diversified recurring mix | Membership, certification, services, reports, grants, training, and pooled assessments |
| I6 | Report-funded launch | Deliberately adverse circular challenger |

I2 and I3 can be composed. The comparison treats them separately so that a strong host cannot hide weak funding and diversified funding cannot hide an unsafe or controlling host.

## 5. Execution

The final run used:

- **23 comparison groups**
- **719 common synthetic histories**
- **2,754 candidate-case evaluations**
- Central, boundary, one-factor, pairwise-corner, and deterministic space-filling cases
- Common-history verification: **passed**
- Stage 3 report revenue tested from **0% to 100% of normalized Stage 3 cost**
- Practical funding replacement concentration tested from **10% to 100%**
- Rights-capacity utilization tested from **25% to 200%**
- Insurance recovery tested from **0% to 100% of the named modeled loss**, before reliability discounts
- Proof-query counts tested from **1 to 100** and prompt repetition from **0 to 12**

The numerical outputs are normalized structural proxies. They are not currency, actuarial estimates, funder probabilities, staff-productivity forecasts, prices, or service promises.

## 6. The Stage 3 funding answer

At the controlled reference with report revenue set to zero, I1 through I4 could each cover a deliberately bounded Stage 3 program when their promised funding was treated as committed and their modeled institutional cost was included:

| Candidate | Stage 3 coverage after ordinary stress | Public-goods coverage | Runway and tail coverage | Gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| I1 | 1.105 | 1.013 | 1.045 | Pass for bounded funding only |
| I2 | 1.139 | 1.147 | 1.118 | Pass |
| I3 | 1.152 | 1.116 | 1.117 | Pass |
| I4 | 1.039 | 1.118 | 1.148 | Pass if added overhead is fully funded |
| I5 | 0.725 | 1.198 | 1.199 | Not a Stage 3 structure |
| I6 | 0.146 | 0.197 | 0.198 | Fail |

This is not a claim that any candidate is actually funded. It shows that the architecture can close without report revenue if the future real budget is prepaid at the required level.

I2 and I3 had the strongest practical Stage 3 balance. I4's separate-entity overhead consumed much of its modeled funding margin. I1 had enough nominal money in the ordinary case but failed the separate funding-quality and independence tests.

## 7. Reports remain upside, not launch collateral

I6 began with only **0.146** of normalized Stage 3 coverage and **0.197–0.198** coverage for public goods and tail obligations.

When report revenue was raised to a full **1.0 times Stage 3 cost**, I6's ordinary operating coverage rose above one. It still failed because public goods and tail coverage remained below **0.20** and because the entire structure depended on revenue that requires the network to exist first.

This is the important economic distinction:

- A paid early analysis may fund its own approved analytical work.
- A contracted report may become evidence of later demand.
- Demonstrated recurring report revenue may support Stage 4.
- None of those may be counted as the money protecting the first pilot's rights, remedies, evaluation, public reporting, or wind-down.

The report business remains strategically valuable. Suite I prevents that value from becoming circular launch financing.

## 8. Funding quality matters more than the headline total

At the controlled reference, usable committed coverage was:

- I1: **0.986**
- I2: **1.088**
- I3: **1.090**
- I4: **1.002**
- I5: **1.102** for its mature operating stage

I1 failed because some nominal support remained revocable or practically controlled by the anchor. I2 and I3 both preserved a margin after enforceability adjustments. I4 barely cleared the usable-funding gate after its additional institutional cost. I5 had the strongest recurring-stage quality profile but is not assumed to exist at launch.

The future financial close must therefore distinguish:

- Cash already transferred
- Protected or restricted cash usable for the named obligation
- Enforceable provider and participant contracts
- Internal staff promises
- Cloud, software, benefit, legal, or distribution credits
- Insurance and indemnity
- Founder and stakeholder labor
- Replacement cost and time
- Withdrawal, step-in, export, and wind-down duties

An impressive sponsor announcement is not a funded right.

## 9. One anchor does not become the lead

I1's controlled practical-control value was **0.213**, but its evaluator evidence share was only **0.521** and usable committed funding remained below one. It failed the controlled independence gate before the most severe stress.

Under the simultaneous sponsor-loss probe, I1 developed a normalized funding gap at only **25% replacement concentration**. Its gap grew to:

- **0.171** at 50% concentration
- **0.317** at 75%
- **0.462** at 100%

I1 may remain a restricted fallback when an anchor prepays the complete capped release and tail, accepts external governance and publication, provides enforceable exit support, and funds replacement of every in-kind dependency. It is not the preferred structure and cannot buy standards ownership, exclusivity, favorable findings, or a public claim of partnership beyond the actual agreement.

## 10. I2 and I3 solve different problems

In the controlled case:

| Measure | I2 diversified funding | I3 hosted project |
|---|---:|---:|
| Usable committed funding | 1.088 | 1.090 |
| Practical control concentration | 0.095 | 0.113 |
| Evaluator direct-evidence share | 0.859 | 0.797 |
| Publication-block proxy | 0.011 | 0.020 |
| In-kind replacement share | 0.070 | 0.063 |
| Founder monthly cap | 16 hours | 10 hours |
| Unavailability continuity | 0.72 | 0.90 |

I2 is better at distributing payer power and protecting independent evidence and publication. I3 is better at reducing founder administration and providing continuity, accounts, contracting, payment, and provider management.

The lead direction is therefore:

**multi-partner and unrestricted funding through a ring-fenced competent host, with independent rights, evaluation, and publication controls.**

A fiscal sponsor is not automatically independent. The host must be diligenced like any other critical operator and must have a documented replacement and exit path.

## 11. An independent entity is a trigger, not a badge

I4 produced the strongest controlled independence values:

- Practical control concentration: **0.063**
- Evaluator evidence share: **0.906**
- Publication-block proxy: **0.005**
- Replacement-cost proxy: **0.038**

But its modeled organization cost reduced ordinary Stage 3 funding coverage to **1.039**, below I2 and I3. Under severe withdrawal, its separate-entity overhead did not protect it from a funding gap when concentration became extreme.

I4 should therefore be chosen when one or more of the following makes a separate entity operationally necessary and the complete overhead is funded:

- Material contracts or employment
- Sensitive or controlled data
- Real-person scoring, credentials, proofs, or benefits
- Independent legal duties that a host cannot carry
- Multi-year operations and reserves
- Certification, appeal, enforcement, or service responsibilities requiring separate authority
- Host conflicts, limits, or exit risk that exceed the cost of independence

The project should not form an entity simply to appear mature. It should not avoid one when real duties require it.

## 12. Capacity is a financial control

The pilot cannot have one generic user cap. It must separately cap:

- Enrolled consumers
- Eligible interactions
- Worker prompts
- Issued and active events
- Active credentials and proofs
- Benefit obligations
- Ordinary explanations and challenges
- Urgent cases and appeals
- Simultaneous incidents
- Corrections, notices, remedies, and exit work

The structural design paused new intake at **90%** of funded capacity. At 85% utilization, modeled post-pause rights completion was **0.879** for I1 and **1.000** for I2 through I4. At 200% demand, I1 fell to **0.721** and failed; I2, I3, and I4 retained **0.833**, **0.825**, and **0.865** respectively because the pause prevented new intake from adding obligations.

These are not service-level targets. They demonstrate why the stop mechanism must fire before the queue reaches the failure point. Expansion follows funded capacity; distribution does not create permission to exceed it.

## 13. Appeals and certification need separate incentive design

I2 through I5 passed the controlled certification and appeal structure because the model required:

- Published assessments paid into the program rather than direct reviewer negotiation
- Independent reviewer assignment, scope, rotation, evidence, and reporting
- No reviewer or staff pay tied to pass, renewal, client satisfaction, affirmance, reversal, denial, or settlement
- Separate funding for initial review, renewal, surveillance, complaints, enforcement, remediation, and retest
- Pooled base appeal capacity plus prospective risk-band adjustment for validated participant-caused workload

At 200% modeled utilization, I2 appeal-fund adequacy fell from **1.100** to **0.550** and failed. Independence language cannot compensate for an overloaded appeal function.

The pooled appeal fund should not charge per successful appeal or adverse outcome. It should establish baseline readiness through prospective role, scale, capacity, and risk bands, then use independently validated repeated defects to change future assessments or recover extraordinary remediation cost under published rules.

## 14. Public goods get a base allocation

The following cannot wait for commercial surplus:

- Consumer and worker participation
- Accessibility and translation
- Open standards and conformance materials
- Independent evaluation
- Mandatory material-harm reporting
- Public-interest research
- Smaller-implementer access
- Challenge, appeal, incident, remedy, and wind-down readiness

The controlled I2, I3, and I4 profiles each funded these obligations above one with report revenue at zero. That becomes a launch condition, not a forecast aspiration.

Commercial surplus may later expand the work. It cannot determine whether the work exists.

## 15. OCA-operated services remain temporary by design

I3 through I5 passed the controlled service-exit comparison only because each modeled service had:

- A documented replacement path
- Separate accounts and access
- Replacement interfaces and export
- A structural review date
- Liquid reserve coverage without insurance
- Wind-down completion at the controlled volume

Controlled replacement-time proxies were approximately **29 days** for I3, **22 days** for I4, and **20 days** for I5. These are comparative values, not promises.

If an early OCA or host runs a registry, portal, synthesis, conformance, or similar service, the service gets no preferred certification, nonpublic standards access, or permanent right to continue. Separate ledgers and predetermined competition, spinout, retirement, or replacement triggers prevent convenience from becoming vertical capture.

## 16. Insurance is recovery, not primary liquidity

At the controlled volume, I3 through I5 closed the named obligation with liquid reserves even when insurance recovery was set to zero. Insurance increased potential recovery but was not needed to satisfy the liquidity gate.

This is the correct order. The future risk-financing model must account for:

- Deductibles
- Exclusions
- Delayed coverage decisions and payment
- Claims-made dates and tail coverage
- Defense control and consent
- Limits shared across several claims
- Insolvent insurer, participant, or indemnitor
- Uninsured discrimination, benefit, regulatory, employment, or contract scenarios
- Cash needed before a claim is resolved

An insurance certificate is not money available to finish an appeal, replace a benefit, notify affected people, or wind down tomorrow.

## 17. Pricing must not tell the system to watch or query more

The safe I4 and I5 structures used predictable base and committed-capacity funding for **88%** and **93%** of their modeled service economics. Both passed from one to 100 proof queries because their marginal usage incentives remained small and no price depended on event direction, score, eligibility, appeal outcome, or benefit value.

The deliberately unsafe per-use challenger failed even at one proof query because it rewarded:

- More proof requests
- Selective or suppressed event issuance
- Consumer retention
- More telemetry
- Rationed correction and rights work

The preferred pricing architecture is therefore:

**predictable base + committed capacity and risk bands + transparent variable-cost reconciliation, with no outcome component.**

Event completeness and correction cannot become financially costly behavior. Consumer rights are not usage features that can be throttled when a budget is exhausted.

## 18. Stage 0 remains intentionally small

The Stage 0 simulation treated founder time as a hard cap, not elastic unpaid capacity.

In the controlled comparison:

- I1 capped Brent at **12 modeled hours per month**
- I2 capped Brent at **16 modeled hours per month**
- I3 capped Brent at **10 modeled hours per month**
- Real-person conduct-data intake remained **zero**
- No operational right or response service level was promised
- Excess demand became backlog, batching, deferral, or a funded-work trigger—not more founder obligation

In the severe unavailability probe, every Stage 0 profile preserved the rule that no consumer right depended on Brent because Stage 0 creates no CQ, event, credential, proof, benefit, appeal, or operating service. I3 had the strongest channel continuity, but continuity means the ability to secure or pause public assets—not informal inheritance of OCA authority.

The Stage 0 operating policy should therefore include:

- A published cadence and response expectation
- Structured intake and batch replies
- A fixed expense and time ceiling
- A stop-doing list
- No bespoke unpaid partner work beyond the cap
- A funded-work trigger for material requests
- No real conduct data or live service
- A trusted emergency contact able to secure or pause domains, repositories, and public channels

## 20. Requirements established by Suite I

1. Stage 3 report revenue is zero in the launch budget.
2. The lead Stage 3 direction composes diversified I2 funding with ring-fenced I3 hosting unless funded operational need justifies I4.
3. The complete planned pilot, severe-but-plausible stress, and post-pilot tail are committed before launch.
4. Funding closes the common budget and every participant-specific budget.
5. Only usable committed support counts; in-kind contributions carry replacement value, term, owner, control, exit, and fallback.
6. Practical concentration covers corporate families across cash, people, infrastructure, data, benefits, distribution, indemnity, and credibility.
7. Appeal, remedy, benefit, evaluation, incident, public-reporting, and wind-down funds are protected from ordinary runway and sponsor retaliation.
8. Every relevant volume has a funded cap, leading indicator, automatic pause, and continuing post-pause obligation.
9. Public goods receive dependable base funding when commercial surplus is zero.
10. Certification uses published program assessments and independently controlled reviewer assignment, scope, rotation, and reporting.
11. Appeals use prospective pooled readiness plus independently attributed risk-band or extraordinary remediation adjustment; no outcome-linked pay.
12. OCA-operated services have separate ledgers, access, review dates, interfaces, export, and replacement or separation paths.
13. Each named material loss has timely liquid funding; insurance is modeled only after exclusions, timing, reliability, and counterparty risk.
14. Pricing uses predictable base and committed-capacity bands with transparent variable-cost reconciliation and no outcome component.
15. Rights, corrections, complete event issuance, and consumer withdrawal are not usage-rationed.
16. Stage 0 remains data-free, bounded, asynchronous, pausable, and free of always-on or consumer-affecting promises.
17. Stage 4 demonstrates diversified recurring coverage before expansion; membership, certification, reports, services, training, and grants remain independently governed.
18. No stage depends on sale of personal conduct data, favorable findings, successful certifications, fines, or unpaid critical labor.

## 21. What remains open

Suite I does not determine:

- The actual Stage 2 or Stage 3 dollar budget
- The first pilot, participant count, volume caps, staffing model, providers, or benefit
- Which funders will commit, on what terms, and at what replacement concentration
- Which fiscal sponsor or host can satisfy the full rights, independence, data, publication, and exit profile
- Whether a Massachusetts entity or another form becomes preferable at the actual trigger
- Exact concentration thresholds by stage, source, sector, role, corporate family, and in-kind dependency
- Real queue distributions, service levels, incident rates, challenge rates, appeal rates, accessibility demand, or remedy exposure
- Certification workload and pricing
- Appeal assessment and risk-band formula
- Insurance coverage, limits, exclusions, deductibles, carriers, reserves, or indemnity
- OCA service make-or-buy decisions and separation triggers
- Mature membership, report, training, service, certification, grant, and assessment revenue
- Report demand, willingness to pay, sales cycle, renewal, margin, or public adoption value
- Brent's exact Stage 0 cadence, time cap, expense cap, support terms, or funded-work threshold

Those are appropriately resolved through a bottom-up budget, partner and host diligence, contracts, provider bids, capacity modeling, insurance and legal review, workload research, and Stage 0 observation.

## 22. Decision and next step

Suite I finds no economic reason to abandon or redirect the Conduct vision.

It does remove three tempting shortcuts:

1. The first major sponsor cannot be allowed to become the system.
2. The future report business cannot be treated as money already earned.
3. Brent's time cannot be treated as the reserve that closes every gap.

The viable path is narrower and healthier:

**bounded founder-led publication → funded contribution and proof → diversified, hosted, fully prepaid pilot → independently governed recurring production.**

Suite J is the final structural suite. It will test whether the claims produced by the pilot survive selection, missingness, wrong denominators, clustering, weak harm detection, multiple analyses, short follow-up, irreproducible partner dashboards, and pressure to report the favorable slice.

Only after Suite J should the project tune final formulas, thresholds, recency periods, confidence rules, transfer values, or public success claims.
