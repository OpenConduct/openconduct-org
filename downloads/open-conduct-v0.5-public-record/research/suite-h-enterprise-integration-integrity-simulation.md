# Conduct Ecosystem — Suite H Enterprise Integration Integrity Simulation Report v0.1

## The answer in human terms

The project can integrate with digital-first platforms and enterprise systems—but no platform should implement the whole system simply because it already owns the customer account, worker interface, transaction data, loyalty program, and benefit engine.

That apparent convenience recreates the exact concentration CQ is meant to avoid. One platform could select the interactions, expose worker participation, import legacy ratings, observe failed proofs, control the source data shown to the evaluator, retain cross-tenant access, block publication, and strand corrections when it exits.

The leading integration is therefore a governed mix:

- An isolated embedded experience for worker and consumer interaction
- Enterprise-side mappings for authoritative source facts and corrections
- Certified webhook or API adapters for minimized real-time exchange
- Periodic signed reconciliation for finalized VCI and missing-event detection
- A proof path isolated from CRM, loyalty, support, marketing, risk, and ordinary analytics
- Direct evaluator access to complete denominators and lifecycle evidence
- A shared correction, incident, rights, and exit protocol across every participating system

This does not make the design less practical. It assigns each system the job it is best positioned to perform without letting any one system become the conduct, identity, proof, evaluation, and governance authority.

## 1. What was tested

Suite H compared:

| ID | Candidate | Role in the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Platform-native integration | Operationally simple full-stack platform candidate |
| H2 | Embedded application | Isolated worker and consumer experience candidate |
| H3 | Webhook or API adapter | Minimized real-time transport and proof-isolation candidate |
| H4 | Enterprise-side adapter | Authoritative source-mapping and correction candidate |
| H5 | Approved periodic VCI batch | Finalized-state and reconciliation backstop |
| H6 | Mixed coalition integration | Governed end-to-end composition |

The full run included:

- 23 scenario-and-mode comparison groups
- 937 unique common synthetic histories
- 1,200 synthetic consumers per history
- 4,568 candidate-case evaluations
- Worker pressure and visibility, source registration and delivery, selective issuance, proof leakage, legacy-feature influence, cross-tenant administration, platform and funder concentration, correction failure, lifecycle latency, rights capacity, and partner exit stress
- Identical generated history for every candidate within each comparison

The preserved result package is `simulation/run_packages/suite-h-v0.1-results.json`.

## 2. No single integration pattern is the system

Each specialized candidate was strongest in a different place:

- H2 best protected the worker-facing interaction.
- H4 best connected CQ to authoritative enterprise source facts and corrections.
- H3 best isolated minimized real-time transport and failed-proof behavior.
- H5 provided the strongest narrow reconciliation and export backstop for finalized VCI.
- H6 produced the strongest controlled end-to-end result by composing those roles.

The conclusion is not “use every technology.” It is that the system needs explicit role boundaries. A production profile should use the smallest number of components that can satisfy all non-compensating gates, while preserving actual administrative and institutional separation.

## 3. H6 becomes the lead end-to-end architecture

In the controlled reference profile, H6 produced:

- Worker-response or abstention visibility to management: **0.042**
- Worker-response distortion proxy: **0.021**
- Evaluator-reconcilable source share: **0.852**
- Failed-proof customer-record risk: **0.000**
- Maximum cross-tenant or dossier reach: **0.116**
- Independent administrative domains: **6**
- Evaluator direct-evidence share: **0.860**
- Practical partner-control proxy: **0.142**
- Correction fan-out completion: **0.872**
- Active-benefit continuity after exit: **0.806**
- Export completeness: **0.900**
- Orphaned-obligation share: **0.194**

These are structural comparison proxies, not a proposed service-level agreement or assurance claim. They show why the mixed architecture leads: it prevents the platform that hosts the experience from automatically controlling source truth, proof visibility, evaluation, reports, and exit.

H6 advances only as a governed composition. If one vendor, cloud, administrator, parent company, key authority, or contractual controller operates all of its nominal modules, it becomes H1 with extra diagrams.

## 4. The mixed architecture has a real coordination cost

H6 does not win for free. It had the highest modeled orchestration complexity. Corrections, reversals, incidents, permissions, credentials, benefits, notices, evaluator evidence, and reports must remain synchronized across several roles.

In the controlled reference case, its cross-provider coordination-failure proxy was **0.024**, higher than the specialized candidates. Under the deliberately hostile operational-failure and exit suite, its end-to-end pass share fell materially.

The system therefore needs:

- One canonical lifecycle and state machine
- Named authority for every transition
- Idempotent, ordered, versioned events
- A complete correction fan-out graph
- End-to-end service levels and pause rules
- Shared incident and collective-correction procedures
- Consumer-visible completion receipts
- Provider replacement, export, and wind-down exercises

The mixed architecture should be rejected if it cannot demonstrate the complete journey. Passing six separate component tests is not enough.

## 5. H2 becomes the lead worker-facing component

An isolated embedded application produced the best controlled worker result among the separately tested worker candidates:

- Management visibility of response or abstention: **0.094**
- Worker-response distortion: **0.048**
- Abstention-privacy strength: **0.765** in the broader bounded cases

The platform may authenticate the worker and provide interaction context, but the CQ component must remain visibly and technically separate from:

- Performance management
- Internal customer notes and tags
- Safety, fraud, quality, sentiment, and productivity systems
- Manager dashboards
- Scheduling, compensation, discipline, and contractor access decisions

No response may be predicted, recommended, preselected, or generated from a transcript. Managers may see aggregate workflow health but not who answered, abstained, expired, disagreed, or challenged.

The surviving H6 profile uses this isolated worker boundary. A platform-native worker workflow that exposes completion or direction does not advance merely because it is easier to deploy.

## 6. H4 becomes the lead source-of-truth component

Enterprise-side adapters were strongest at mapping CQ to the business systems that actually know whether a responsibility was created, changed, fulfilled, disputed, reversed, or caused by the business.

In the controlled reference profile, H4 produced:

- Evaluator-reconcilable source share: **0.890**
- Unreconciled source share: **0.110**
- Outcome-selection-bias proxy: **0.007**

That does not authorize a business to define its own conduct semantics. The enterprise retains source facts and detailed evidence, while the certified mapping manifest defines:

- The eligible source universe
- The exact source field and operational meaning
- The responsibility unit and notice
- Consumer, business, shared, and external causation
- Finalization, exceptions, disputes, correction, and reversal
- Which minimized assertion may leave the enterprise
- What the independent evaluator can reconcile

H4 should issue VCI assertions only from allowlisted mappings. Internal fields such as difficult, VIP, fraud risk, sentiment, cost to serve, complaint, refund, no-show, escalation, loyalty tier, or high maintenance have no CQ meaning by themselves.

## 7. H3 becomes the lead real-time exchange and proof-isolation component

The webhook or API adapter created the strongest separately tested boundary for minimized exchange and failed-proof privacy.

In the controlled reference profile, H3 produced:

- Failed-proof visibility: **0.000**
- Failed-proof customer-record risk: **0.000**
- Cross-tenant or dossier reach: **0.138**
- Independent administrative domains: **5**
- Evaluator direct-evidence share: **0.768**
- Export completeness: **0.844**

H3 is not sufficient by itself. Its controlled evaluator-reconcilable source share was **0.736**, below the comparison gate. An adapter sees what the source chooses to emit; it cannot prove what was omitted before emission.

The surviving pattern therefore combines H3 transport with H4 source mapping and H5-style reconciliation. The adapter must support signatures, scoped authorization, idempotency, ordering, retries, quarantine, correction references, key rotation, and explicit tenant consent without collecting a broad enterprise dataset.

## 8. H5 is a required VCI backstop, not an end-to-end option

Periodic signed batch exchange is useful for:

- Reconciliation against the full eligible source universe
- Finalized VCI states that do not require real-time issuance
- Detecting missing, delayed, duplicated, rejected, orphaned, corrected, and reversed events
- Export and migration
- Rebuilding a privacy-safe audit state after an outage or provider change

In the controlled reference profile, H5 produced:

- Evaluator-reconcilable source share: **0.796**
- Outcome-selection-bias proxy: **0.007**
- Export completeness: **0.900**
- Active-benefit continuity: **0.777**

H5 cannot create a historical NCS response, run an interactive proof, or provide the worker experience. Batch is restricted to approved finalized VCI and reconciliation. It cannot become a bulk legacy import or a convenient substitute for lifecycle updates.

## 9. A platform-native full stack does not advance

H1 was operationally attractive and produced strong controlled source coverage. But its concentration overwhelmed that advantage:

- Worker-response or abstention visibility: **0.346**
- Failed-proof visibility even without injected leakage: **0.064**
- Failed-proof customer-record risk: **0.042**
- Cross-tenant or dossier reach: **0.581**
- Evaluator direct-evidence share: **0.416**
- Export completeness after exit: **0.516**
- Active-benefit continuity after exit: **0.371**
- Orphaned-obligation share: **0.629**

The platform-native full-stack candidate is rejected as the governing end-to-end architecture.

This does not prohibit native platform experiences. A platform may implement an approved worker screen, consumer screen, source mapping, verifier, or benefit action. It may not use that role to control the evaluator, reports, proof outcomes, rights, cross-company synthesis, standards, or continuation of the system.

## 10. Failed-proof privacy has zero tolerance

H3 and H6 preserved zero failed-proof visibility in the clean controlled case. A modeled leak of only 0.1% per observed channel produced cumulative visibility of approximately **0.005** and failed the invariant for both.

At a 1% injected leak, visibility reached approximately **0.049**. At 5%, it reached **0.226**.

This confirms the Suite E finding: removing a failure reason from the API response is not enough. The complete integration must test:

- Response codes and timing
- Network calls and callbacks
- Request, support, and error logs
- CRM and customer timelines
- Loyalty and benefit records
- Data warehouses and analytics
- Frontline and manager screens
- Retry, outage, and fallback behavior

Only an authorized successful Eligible presentation creates a CQ-specific enterprise record. Everything else follows the same ordinary baseline path.

## 11. Source completeness must be measured before transmission

A valid signed event proves that one event was sent by an authorized issuer. It does not prove that all eligible events were registered or that inconvenient outcomes were not omitted.

Every production integration therefore needs a privacy-minimized source-population ledger or equivalent evidence covering:

- Eligible interactions
- Registration before outcome
- Sampling and prompt selection
- Prompt delivery and response state
- Responsibility creation and lifecycle
- Issuance, rejection, delay, quarantine, dispute, correction, reversal, and omission
- Source outages and schema changes

The evaluator receives the denominators and exception evidence directly—not a partner-curated success extract. Missingness can invalidate an issuer or event class without making absence an adverse consumer record.

## 12. The launch remains prospectively clean

Every production candidate held legacy influence at zero because the prospective gate remained active. The counterfactual showed the bypass risk at the highest tested legacy influence:

- H1 platform-native contamination: **1.000**
- H2 embedded contamination: **0.420**
- H3 isolated adapter contamination: **0.120**
- H4 enterprise-side contamination: **0.630**

The clean API payload is not enough. Legacy ratings, notes, loyalty, value, fraud, risk, safety, sentiment, complaints, enforcement, and customer tiers cannot influence recruitment, sampling, prompts, investigations, proof requests, benefits, interfaces, evaluator datasets, or downstream treatment.

Historical NCS backfill remains prohibited. Historical VCI requires its own source, responsibility, correction, fairness, notice, authorization, and legal profile.

## 13. Administrative reality can collapse a modular design

Separate tenants, services, databases, or cloud accounts do not establish separation when one support team, parent company, warehouse, key authority, backup operator, or superadministrator can access them all.

The controlled reference cross-tenant or dossier reach was:

- H1: **0.581**
- H2: **0.245**
- H3: **0.138**
- H4: **0.317**
- H5: **0.094**
- H6: **0.116**

The complete privilege graph must cover legal control, processors, subprocessors, clouds, keys, support, backups, analytics, incidents, legal response, bulk export, and provider exit. A mixed integration passes only if its independent domains are real.

## 14. The evaluator cannot depend on the platform's export

In the controlled partner-control comparison, evaluator direct-evidence share was:

- H1: **0.416**
- H2: **0.601**
- H3: **0.768**
- H4: **0.740**
- H6: **0.860**

The evaluator needs contractual and technical access to the eligible source universe, configuration, lifecycle, missingness, proof, benefit, baseline, challenge, correction, and incident evidence necessary for the preregistered analysis.

The platform may review its own factual or security information during a fixed window. It cannot select the evaluator dataset, remove inconvenient periods, block a finding, terminate access before final reporting, or make evidence depend on continued sponsorship.

## 15. Correction completion is an end-to-end state

A source-system correction is incomplete while an inaccurate effect remains in any active:

- Issuer event
- Synthesis state
- Consumer view
- Credential or proof eligibility
- Permission
- Future benefit path
- Evaluator dataset
- Aggregate report
- Notice, challenge, or remedy record

The controlled H6 profile achieved correction fan-out completion of **0.872**. That is still not a production threshold. The actual system needs a per-harm completion definition, service level, pause rule, consumer receipt, and collective-correction mechanism.

A valid completed benefit is not clawed back because a later correction changes future eligibility. When an inaccurate event wrongly prevented a benefit, the remedy path handles replacement or compensation.

## 16. Exit must work before launch

The strongest controlled exit result came from H6, with:

- Active-benefit continuity: **0.806**
- Export completeness: **0.900**
- Clean deletion and final-report completion: **0.882**
- Orphaned obligations: **0.194**

Capacity still controlled survival. In the isolated H6 capacity probe:

- At 100% utilization, correction completion remained **0.872** and the exit gate passed.
- At 125%, correction completion fell to **0.698** and the gate failed.
- At 200%, correction completion fell to **0.436** and orphaned obligations rose to **0.564**.

The pilot needs export schemas, replacement providers, keys, funds, evidence rights, benefit commitments, correction capacity, consumer and worker communications, deletion behavior, and final reporting that survive withdrawal of the largest platform.

## 17. Production conformance is a journey, not a component checklist

An adapter can validate schemas and signatures while the real system still fails because:

- A worker prompt is visible to a manager.
- An eligible interaction was never registered.
- A failed proof enters the CRM through a callback.
- A correction remains stale in a credential or report.
- A benefit fails at one location.
- A shared administrator can export every tenant.
- The evaluator receives only a curated extract.
- Platform exit strands an appeal or completed-benefit obligation.

Certification must execute production-shaped journeys across platform, business, worker, consumer, evaluator, appeal, verifier, benefit, report, incident, correction, and exit boundaries. It expires or reopens after material ownership, role, schema, model, benefit, vendor, data-flow, key, or configuration change.

## 18. Candidate verdicts

| Candidate | Suite H verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Platform-native | **Reject as full-stack architecture; restrict to certified components** | Strong workflow and source access, but unacceptable concentration, leakage, evaluator dependence, and exit risk |
| H2: Embedded application | **Lead worker and consumer interface component** | Best separately tested worker privacy; host observation and source completeness still require external boundaries |
| H3: Webhook or API adapter | **Lead minimized real-time exchange and proof component** | Strong proof isolation, portability, custody, and exit; cannot prove pre-transmission completeness alone |
| H4: Enterprise-side adapter | **Lead source mapping and correction component** | Strongest source truth and lifecycle connection; local legacy, worker, proof, and administrative risks remain |
| H5: Periodic VCI batch | **Required narrow reconciliation and export component** | Valuable finalized-state backstop; cannot support NCS, interactive proof, or the whole journey |
| H6: Mixed coalition | **Lead end-to-end architecture** | Best controlled balance across all boundaries; survives only with real separation and exercised coordination |

No candidate is approved for a real platform, company, vendor, or production deployment.

## 19. What remains unresolved

Suite H cannot establish:

- Which platform will grant the required application, API, webhook, source, evaluator, and exit permissions
- The exact pilot integration and its actual vendors, administrators, clouds, keys, contracts, and data flows
- Source-registration, delivery, correction, proof-leakage, latency, availability, and service-level thresholds
- Worker comprehension, voluntariness, compensation, accessibility, retaliation safety, labor authority, or consultation requirements
- The lawful role and data authority of each platform, business, issuer, verifier, evaluator, wallet, report function, and OCA
- Whether an integration can resist auxiliary-data and insider attacks
- Whether a business will accept direct evaluator evidence rights and independent publication
- Whether correction, incident, rights, and exit capacity can be funded at the selected pilot volume
- Production security, privacy, accessibility, labor, consumer-reporting, competition, and sector compliance

These require production-shaped synthetic prototypes, confidential partner discovery, source-system and privilege mapping, worker and consumer research, end-to-end threat testing, direct evaluator access, legal review, and exercised correction and exit.

## 20. Decision and next step

The digital-first pilot strategy remains sound. Uber, Airbnb, Resy, Toast, customer-support platforms, and comparable companies are attractive because they already have accounts, interaction records, employee workflows, APIs, benefits, and partner networks.

But their maturity creates leverage, not automatic trust. The strongest partner is not the company willing to put CQ everywhere fastest. It is the company willing to let CQ remain prospectively clean, technically separable, independently evaluated, privacy-preserving, correctable, portable, and able to survive the company's departure.

The enterprise architecture becomes:

**isolated experience + authoritative local source + minimized certified exchange + independent proof and evaluation + periodic reconciliation + governed correction and exit.**

Suite H shows that this is structurally possible. The next structural comparison is Suite I: whether the organization can pay for the complete rights, independence, provider, incident, and wind-down burden without becoming controlled by one sponsor, one platform, report revenue, certification incentives, or unpaid founder labor.
