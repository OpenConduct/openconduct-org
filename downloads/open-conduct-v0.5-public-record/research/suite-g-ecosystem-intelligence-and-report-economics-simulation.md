# Conduct Ecosystem — Suite G Ecosystem Intelligence and Report Economics Simulation Report v0.1

## The answer in human terms

The alliance can create valuable reports that no individual member can create—but the product must be **governed intelligence, not data access**.

The alliance's defensible asset is a network lens made from:

- A common standard and metric dictionary
- Required, auditable data contribution across participating sources
- Comparable cross-company and cross-context cohorts
- Independent analytical methods and publication authority
- Privacy-preserving computation across data that remains distributed
- The ability to explain what one member's own results mean against a protected network benchmark

The alliance should not sell a portal in which a company asks arbitrary questions of the network. That model reconstructs people and competitors, encourages favorable slicing, creates a data-broker relationship, and lets revenue pressure determine what the system collects.

The leading architecture is a governed hybrid:

- Secure aggregation for repeatable public and benchmark statistics where it can answer the question
- Fixed, versioned federated queries for approved recurring analyses
- Controlled research environments only for exceptional work, with no ordinary access to network person-level synthesis
- Thresholded aggregate snapshots for specific published outputs, not an indefinitely sliceable central warehouse
- Member diagnostics that combine the member's own detailed data with protected network benchmarks

Unsafe questions receive no answer. That fail-closed behavior is not a product defect. It is what keeps the report business from becoming the surveillance business the project is designed to avoid.

## 1. What was tested

Suite G compared:

| ID | Candidate | Role in the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Central thresholded aggregate warehouse | Simple fixed-output and longitudinal aggregate candidate |
| G2 | Federated approved queries | Practical distributed-query candidate |
| G3 | Secure aggregation or multiparty computation | Strong privacy-preserving recurring-statistics candidate |
| G4 | Controlled research environment or clean room | Deep-analysis and exceptional research candidate |
| G5 | Hybrid architecture by report family | Governed routing candidate |
| G6 | Open-ended member query | Adversarial self-service candidate expected to fail |

The full run included:

- 14 scenario-and-mode comparison groups
- 730 unique common synthetic histories
- 1,200 synthetic consumers per history
- 3,956 candidate-case evaluations
- Minimum cell size, repeated query count, contributor and issuer dominance, source correlation, administrative access, funding concentration, report-revenue pressure, correction failure, population shift, and site heterogeneity stress
- Identical generated history for every candidate within each comparison

The preserved result package is `simulation/run_packages/suite-g-v0.1-results.json`.

## 2. The report business is viable only as a governed product

Suite G supports the basic commercial thesis: shared definitions, cross-participant coverage, and independent analysis can produce insight that one company cannot generate from its own loyalty, support, operations, or customer records.

For a Delta-like member, the report product can answer questions such as:

- Where do the member's own operating conditions create avoidable human friction relative to a sufficiently broad cohort?
- Which company-caused failures are being misread as customer conduct?
- Which interaction functions, channels, or operating conditions diverge from an approved benchmark?
- Which interventions improve both operational follow-through and the human experience without degrading ordinary service?
- Where are missingness, challenges, corrections, reversals, or benefit failures indicating a system problem?

It cannot answer:

- Which named competitor has the strongest or weakest customers
- What United knows about a particular traveler
- Which consumers, workers, locations, or incidents belong to a hidden segment
- How to create a marketing, risk, loyalty, fraud, or service-treatment audience from CQ data
- Any new question a paying member can formulate merely because it is willing to pay

That boundary preserves both the business value and the project's original purpose.

## 3. G5 becomes the lead report architecture

G5 assigns each approved report family to the least revealing architecture capable of answering it. In the controlled reference case—20-person minimum cells, 20 cumulative queries, 25% maximum contributor and issuer share, and moderate funding and report pressure—it produced:

- Counterfactual person-reconstruction risk before suppression: **0.038**
- Counterfactual competitor-inference risk before suppression: **0.068**
- Privacy-safe member-value proxy: **0.732**
- Usable coverage across all four modeled report families: **4 of 4**
- Publication-block risk: **0.035**
- Report-driven collection-drift risk: **0.051**

Those values are structural comparison proxies, not real privacy guarantees or demand estimates. Their importance is comparative: G5 retained the broadest useful portfolio while keeping person and competitor exposure low and preserving the ability to refuse unsafe outputs.

The hybrid must be a routing constitution, not a menu from which a client selects the least restrictive tool. Each report family needs a predetermined approved architecture, purpose, cohort, query budget, retention rule, and output review.

## 4. Secure aggregation is the preferred recurring-statistics engine

G3 had the lowest bounded-administration report-data reach at **0.038**, no retained person-level rows, and the lowest modeled collection-drift risk among the protected candidates.

In the controlled reference case it produced:

- Person-reconstruction risk before suppression: **0.023**
- Competitor-inference risk before suppression: **0.045**
- Privacy-safe member-value proxy: **0.473**
- Coverage of **3 of 4** report families

It remained structurally usable at 1,000 cumulative approved computations in the controlled probe, while G1, G2, and G4 reached their query budgets and stopped. This does not mean unlimited secure queries are safe. Query composition, cohort dominance, collusion, auxiliary data, and the released statistics themselves still require a cumulative privacy budget and output review.

G3 is strongest for repeatable ecosystem statistics and broad benchmarks. It is weaker for exploratory member diagnostics and complex research. That is why it is a core component rather than the complete report system.

## 5. Federated approved queries are the practical early path

G2 provides a credible way to begin without central raw-data custody. Certified participants run fixed, versioned queries locally and return only approved thresholded results and audit evidence.

In the controlled reference case it produced:

- Person-reconstruction risk before suppression: **0.072**
- Competitor-inference risk before suppression: **0.116**
- Privacy-safe member-value proxy: **0.548**
- Coverage of **3 of 4** report families

Its bounded report-data reach was **0.094**, and its modeled correction-and-withdrawal integrity was stronger than G1, G3, or G4.

G2 advances as the most practical early reporting mechanism only when:

- Queries are approved, signed, versioned, and purpose-bound
- Members cannot choose definitions, exclusions, dates, or cohorts
- Contribution completeness is independently reconcilable
- Repeated and colluding queries share one cumulative budget
- Source custodians cannot return person-level CQ synthesis
- Corrections and withdrawals propagate into future outputs
- Unsafe outputs are suppressed

Federated does not mean trustworthy. A participant can still omit inconvenient inputs or run a valid query over a manipulated source population.

## 6. Aggregate warehouses are useful only for bounded snapshots

G1 performed well for simple fixed reports and had the strongest modeled rerun equivalence. It also had structural weaknesses:

- Persistent aggregate custody of **365 days** in the comparison profile
- Bounded report-data reach of **0.190**
- Lower contribution-completeness proxy than G2–G5
- Higher publication-block and collection-drift risk than the lead architecture
- Rapid loss of safe answerability as queries, slices, or dominant contributors increased

At 20 controlled queries, G1 remained usable. At 100, it suppressed the output. This is the correct response: many individually safe aggregates can become a reconstructable history when retained and combined.

G1 therefore advances only as a restricted output pattern:

- A fixed report snapshot or small set of noncomposable aggregate tables
- Approved cohort and metric definitions
- Version and correction history
- Complementary suppression and cumulative composition review
- Expiration or archival controls that prevent the warehouse from becoming an internal open-ended query surface

The alliance may hold its own published aggregate report outputs. It should not build a permanent warehouse of endlessly sliceable network aggregates merely because no row is labeled as personal data.

## 7. Clean rooms are exceptional, not the member product

G4 supplied the greatest controlled analytical flexibility. In the reference case it covered all four report families and produced a member-value proxy of **0.638**. But that flexibility came with the largest protected-candidate privacy and custody burden:

- Bounded report-data reach: **0.237**
- Retained person-level-row proxy: **0.350**
- Bounded single-operator failure radius: **0.401**
- One-operator network-view marker in **38.5%** of broad custody stress cases
- Reproducibility privacy cost: **0.475**, compared with **0.020** for G5

G4 is therefore restricted to exceptional independent research or member diagnostics that use the member's own detailed data plus a protected network benchmark. It is not an ordinary self-service environment for network microdata, person-level CQ synthesis, worker responses, complaint narratives, or proof history.

Every controlled environment needs a named research purpose, approved fields, analyst eligibility, output review, short retention, complete logs, correction and deletion behavior, independent publication rights, and an executable shutdown path.

## 8. Open-ended member queries are rejected

G6 offered apparent flexibility by answering every request. That is exactly why it failed.

Across the full stress suite:

- It released an answer in **100%** of cases.
- It retained audience-creation capability in **100%** of cases.
- Median person-reconstruction risk was **0.790**.
- Median competitor-inference risk reached **1.000**.
- Median publication-block risk was **0.630**.
- Median collection-drift risk was **0.700**.

At 1,000 controlled queries, its person-reconstruction risk reached **0.827**. At 75% contributor and issuer dominance, its competitor-inference risk reached **1.000**.

G6 is rejected. The alliance cannot sell an API, dashboard, clean-room seat, analyst interface, or premium membership tier that lets a customer formulate arbitrary network questions. A human analyst taking requests by email is still an open-ended query service if the purpose and safeguards are not fixed in advance.

## 9. Suppression is part of the product

The deliberately hostile design included small cells, narrow slices, repeated queries, and dominant contributors. The protected candidates often refused to release an answer:

| Candidate | Output released across the full hostile suite | Unsafe-output suppression |
|---|---:|---:|
| G1 | 2.3% | 97.7% |
| G2 | 7.2% | 92.8% |
| G3 | 25.8% | 74.2% |
| G4 | 1.0% | 99.0% |
| G5 | 25.8% | 74.2% |
| G6 | 100.0% | 0.0% |

These are not forecasts of how often a real report would be unavailable. The suite intentionally concentrates unsafe cases. The comparison shows the required behavior: protected architectures lose coverage; the open query architecture converts unsafe conditions into apparent product value.

The report product, membership promise, and sales process must say plainly that some questions cannot be answered and some cuts cannot be shown.

## 10. Cell size alone is not privacy

In the controlled cell-size probe:

- G1–G5 all suppressed at cell sizes of 1, 3, and 5.
- G3 and G5 could answer at 10 under the other controlled conditions.
- G1, G2, and G4 required at least 20 in their comparison profiles.
- G6 answered at every cell size and retained high reconstruction risk.

No universal minimum is selected by Suite G. The real rule needs minimum cell size plus:

- Minimum independent issuer and participant count
- Maximum contributor and corporate-family share
- Rarity and sensitivity review
- Location, role, function, and time-window limits
- Complementary suppression
- Protection against differencing and repeated queries
- Auxiliary-data and collusion testing

A cell of 50 can still expose a named competitor if one company contributed nearly all 50 observations.

## 11. Dominant contributors can defeat every aggregation technology

At 50% contributor and issuer dominance in the controlled probe, G2, G3, and G5 still cleared their modeled release gates, though competitor-inference risk had increased. G1 and G4 suppressed. At 75% dominance, every protected candidate suppressed. G6 continued to answer.

The key finding is architectural: secure computation can hide individual inputs while the released aggregate still reveals a dominant participant. Cryptography does not create a real cohort.

Every report requires:

- A maximum contributor and corporate-family dominance cap
- Enough materially independent participants
- No named or readily inferable competitor result
- A rule for how mergers, exits, and source withdrawal change the cohort
- Suppression rather than noise or relabeling when the cohort is not defensible

## 12. Contribution completeness is a condition of membership

Reports are not credible if members contribute only favorable locations, interactions, periods, channels, event classes, or corrections. Secure aggregation of selected data is still selected data.

Participating data contributors therefore need enforceable duties covering:

- The eligible source universe and completeness reconciliation
- Stable metric and event definitions
- Required correction, reversal, and withdrawal propagation
- Disclosure of missingness, exclusions, outages, and known source changes
- Independent conformance evidence
- No withdrawal or restatement designed to erase an unfavorable published period
- Preservation of reproducible aggregate evidence when a participant exits, subject to rights and law

A member may leave the alliance. It may not rewrite what the governed network accurately observed while it participated.

## 13. Member diagnostics use the member's data, not everyone else's

The most commercially promising member product is a diagnostic that combines:

1. The member's own detailed operational and customer data, still controlled by that member.
2. Approved OCA metric definitions and analytical methods.
3. A sufficiently broad protected network benchmark.
4. Independent interpretation and practical recommendations.

That product can tell a business something it does not know without disclosing a competitor or person. Paid depth may include additional approved report families, cadence, analyst time, workshops, and implementation guidance. It cannot include smaller cells, raw access, flexible cohort selection, additional personal fields, or preferential standards influence.

## 14. Material harm cannot remain a confidential client finding

Every architecture showed some susceptibility to funding, member, or report-revenue pressure. G5 had the strongest structural publication support, but no data architecture can guarantee institutional courage.

Before the first commissioned report, OCA needs a publication rule that separates:

- Ordinary member-confidential operational findings
- Aggregated public-interest and ecosystem-health findings
- Material evidence of safety, discrimination, retaliation, baseline degradation, systemic error, security failure, rights failure, or governance capture

Material harm requires independent escalation. A member should receive a defined verification and responsible-response window, but it cannot purchase permanent confidentiality, alter the conclusion, choose the cohort, or delay publication until the finding becomes commercially convenient.

## 15. What OCA can sell

OCA can charge for:

- Governed benchmark reports
- Member-specific diagnostics using the member's own data
- Recurring industry and interaction-function reports
- Additional approved report cadence and depth
- Analyst interpretation and executive briefings
- Workshops, training, and implementation guidance
- Sponsored public research with disclosed funding and independent methods and publication

OCA cannot charge for:

- Raw or person-level network data
- Arbitrary query access
- Named competitor intelligence
- Smaller cells or weaker privacy rules
- Suppressed public-interest findings
- Favorable methods, conclusions, certification, or standards influence
- A data feed for treatment, marketing, loyalty, fraud, risk, workforce, or advertising systems

The paid product is the analytical work and governed interpretation. The shared method definitions, cohort rules, limitations, and public-interest findings must remain sufficiently open for credibility.

## 16. Reports do not fund Stage 3 into existence

Suite G cannot demonstrate willingness to pay or forecast report revenue. Its value proxies show that privacy-safe useful products are structurally possible; they do not establish that a business will buy them at the required price.

Stage 3 therefore retains the existing rule:

- Report revenue is set to zero in the funded pilot base case.
- Early report prototypes may recover their own governed analytical cost.
- Only contracted and demonstrated lawful revenue enters Stage 4 base forecasts.
- Rights, appeal, evaluation, public-interest reporting, security, and wind-down do not depend on one report client renewing.
- Revenue must not cause broader collection, longer retention, more queries, or weaker suppression.

The network must exist before the network lens becomes uniquely valuable. Report intelligence is a destination and recurring engine—not bridge financing assumed into existence.

## 17. Reproducibility does not require a central dossier

Median governed-rerun equivalence across the broad reproducibility stress was:

- G1: **0.793**
- G2: **0.742**
- G3: **0.692**
- G4: **0.658**
- G5: **0.768**

G1 benefited from retaining stable aggregate snapshots, while G2–G5 depended more on source availability, correction propagation, computation reproducibility, and governed versioning. G5 provided the strongest modeled correction-and-withdrawal integrity at **0.721** while retaining low reproducibility privacy cost.

Every published or paid output requires:

- A versioned metric, cohort, source, query, and suppression manifest
- Funding and client disclosure
- Missingness, uncertainty, and limitations
- Correction and restatement policy
- A privacy-safe reproducibility package
- Preservation of the original output and reason for any change

The goal is not to reproduce an obsolete error forever. It is to explain exactly what was known, calculated, corrected, and published at each version.

## 18. Candidate verdicts

| Candidate | Suite G verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| G1: Central aggregate warehouse | **Restricted output pattern** | Useful for fixed aggregate snapshots; persistent composition and central-control risk prevent general warehouse use |
| G2: Federated approved queries | **Lead practical early component** | Strong distributed reporting value when queries and source completeness are governed |
| G3: Secure aggregation | **Preferred recurring-statistics component** | Lowest protected data reach and drift; limited analytical flexibility |
| G4: Controlled research environment | **Restricted exceptional component** | High diagnostic value but materially greater row access, custody, and failure radius |
| G5: Hybrid by report family | **Lead portfolio architecture** | Best combination of privacy-safe member value, report-family coverage, and institutional safeguards |
| G6: Open-ended member query | **Rejected** | Converts report access into person, worker, competitor, audience, and mission-drift capability |

No candidate is approved for real data or commercial operation.

## 19. What remains unresolved

Suite G cannot establish:

- Which exact reports businesses will value or buy
- Willingness to pay, pricing, packaging, sales cycle, renewal, or gross margin
- The final federated-query, secure-aggregation, clean-room, or privacy technology
- Real contribution completeness, cohort quality, correction, and source-exit behavior
- Exact cell, cohort, issuer-count, dominance, rarity, time-window, privacy-budget, or suppression thresholds
- Whether auxiliary data can reidentify a person, worker, incident, location, or competitor
- The cross-border, privacy, competition, antitrust, labor, consumer-reporting, trade-secret, database, and research-law profile
- The operating form and practical independence of the Research and Intelligence Office
- Whether a member accepts an answer it cannot inspect down to raw data
- Whether OCA can publish a material adverse finding against a major payer in practice

These require member discovery, public-interest and researcher discovery, report prototypes using synthetic and then appropriately governed data, privacy attacks, legal review, pricing tests, contracts, and an exercised publication process.

## 20. Decision and next step

OCA can have a real and potentially powerful report business without becoming the infrastructure data company.

Its moat is not secret custody of everyone's history. Its moat is the combination of a shared standard, comparable governed contribution, independent analysis, privacy-preserving network computation, trusted publication, and the accumulated institutional knowledge required to explain the ecosystem responsibly.

The commercial promise is: **we can tell a participating business what its own systems cannot see, using a protected network benchmark no single participant controls.**

The constitutional limit is: **we sell reports, interpretation, and governed insight—not people, competitors, audiences, or access to the network.**

Suite G preserves the proposed revenue engine while making its boundaries enforceable. Its report and data-separation constraints supplied the now-complete Suite H enterprise-integration comparison.
