# EDITORIAL.md — Open Conduct (openconduct.org)

> **The editorial rules for every word on openconduct.org.**
> `AGENTS.md` §9 covers voice across both repositories. This file governs copy on the standards site specifically, and it wins here if the two disagree.
> The consumer site has its own file with different rules. Do not apply those rules to this site, or this site's rules to that one.

---

## 1. Who is reading

Someone who came to evaluate the work, not to be sold on it. A privacy lawyer. A labor representative. A disability advocate. A measurement researcher. A regulator's staffer. An engineer at a company deciding whether this is credible. A journalist looking for the hole. A hostile critic hoping to find one.

They share three traits:

- **They are looking for the flaw.** That is the job they came to do, and the site should help them do it.
- **They will not be charmed.** Warmth reads as spin to this audience. Enthusiasm reads as bias.
- **They can handle density.** Length is fine. Vagueness is not.

**The test for every page:** could a hostile expert use this page to find something wrong with the proposal? If the page makes that harder, the page is failing.

---

## 2. What this site has to do

1. **State what the proposal actually requires**, precisely enough to be implemented or refuted.
2. **Show what remains unresolved**, in the same breath as what is settled.
3. **Never claim more than the evidence supports.**
4. **Make challenge easy** — every page should leave the reader knowing where to push.

The most credible content on this site is the list of things that are still open. Treat it as the asset, not the embarrassment.

---

## 3. The structural rule

**Precision first. Warmth never.**

conduct.is may open aspirationally because it is trying to interest a stranger. This site opens with what the page establishes. A reader here has already decided to pay attention; the job is to be worth it.

Every section states what it contains. Every claim carries its status. Every unresolved question is named as unresolved rather than skipped.

---

## 4. Headlines

- **Up to fourteen words.** Longer than the consumer site, because descriptive beats punchy here.
- **Say what the section establishes**, not what the reader should feel about it.
- **No metaphor. Anywhere.** There is no aspirational hero on this site, so the exception that applies to conduct.is does not apply here.
- **A number in a heading is good.** "Six choices doing most of the legal work" tells the reader the shape of what follows.
- **No headline that describes the page.** "What the current proposal requires" says nothing.

---

## 5. Sentences

The banned constructions from the consumer site are banned here too, because they are bad writing rather than wrong-audience writing:

| Pattern | Why it fails here too |
|---|---|
| "It's not X, it's Y" and "no longer X, now Y" | Rhetorical shape standing in for an argument. |
| Em dash bolting on a "not X" clause | Makes a qualification look like an afterthought when it is load-bearing. |
| Three parallel clauses in a row | Cadence borrowed from marketing. |
| Denying a service nobody imagined | "This project does not operate a score-dispute desk." |
| A truism about the project's own process | Wastes the attention of someone who came to read the substance. |
| Verbless fragment as a heading | "What does not." |
| AI filler | "at its core," "this represents," "this underscores." |

**Two additions specific to this site:**

- **Never soften a limit to make the proposal sound better.** A prohibition stated weakly is worse than no prohibition, because it invites the reader to test it.
- **Never harden a hypothesis to make it sound stronger.** A candidate written as though settled is the single fastest way to lose this audience.

**Noun piles are permitted here** where the list is the content. A jurisdiction matrix or an enumerated set of gates is legitimately a list. On conduct.is that would be jargon; here it is precision. Use judgement: if the reader needs every item, keep every item.

---

## 6. Whose point of view

**Third person, and role nouns are correct.** Consumer, worker, verifier, issuer, participating business, receiving business. These are defined roles in the specification and using them precisely is the point. This is the exact opposite of the consumer site, which uses "you" throughout.

**The specification is the subject, not the author.** "The standard requires" rather than "we require." The founder appears by name only where stewardship, authorship, or accountability is genuinely the topic.

**Do not address the reader as a supporter.** They may not be one.

---

## 7. Status language, and why it is the most important rule here

Every substantive claim carries its standing. The site already uses two vocabularies and both must stay visible.

**Artifact status:** Current · Candidate · Evidence-only · Historical · Superseded

**Decision class:** CONSTITUTIONAL · REQUIRED FOR COHERENCE · CANDIDATE · DEFERRED · REJECTED

Rules:

- A **candidate** must never be written in the voice of a settled rule.
- **Evidence-only** material must never imply real-world validation. Synthetic testing shows whether a structure holds together and nothing else.
- **Superseded** and **historical** material stays available and stays labeled.
- Where the site and the versioned record disagree, **the record controls**, and the page should say so.

Normative keywords on specification pages follow their ordinary standards meaning: MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, MAY. Do not use them decoratively in editorial copy.

---

## 8. Citations and evidence

- A claim about law, research, precedent, or another organization's behavior **carries a source**.
- Name the instrument, not the vibe. "Article 5 of the EU AI Act" rather than "European regulation."
- **Distinguish what an instrument says from what it means for this proposal.** The second is analysis and must be labeled as such.
- Legal writing on this site is **issue-spotting, never advice**, and every page carrying it says so.
- Never cite the volume of the project's own work as evidence that the project works.

---

## 9. Naming other companies

**Permitted here, unlike on conduct.is**, where the specification already uses a company as a category example and the reference is factual and durable.

Rules that still hold:

- Never imply a partnership, endorsement, active discussion, or commitment.
- Where a company is named as one example of a replaceable category, say that explicitly.
- Prefer the category when the specific name adds nothing.

---

## 10. Honesty that cannot be edited away

Every page keeps these true:

- Nothing operates. No score, no network, no alliance, no certification, no pilot, no partner.
- The Open Conduct Alliance is proposed. It does not exist.
- One person stewards this today, and a live system would require independent governance.
- Synthetic testing establishes structure, not validity, fairness, legality, or demand.
- No claim of being fair, valid, lawful, secure, or production-ready until evidence supports it.

State each limit once, precisely, where it belongs. Do not attach a hedge to every sentence — a page that qualifies everything communicates nothing.

## 11. Worked examples

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What the current proposal requires. | Four rules the design will not bend on. | A heading that said nothing became one that previews the shape. |
| A limited business result | One answer for the business | Noun phrase became a statement. |
| Conduct should stop where existing incentive systems begin. | Conduct decides who qualifies. Something else hands out the reward. | Abstract boundary became two concrete actors. |
| Each label shows what an artifact can—and cannot—establish. | What each label tells you, and what it does not. | Em dash bolt-on removed, jargon noun removed. |
| The minimum gate is larger than a working calculation. | A working formula is the smallest part of this. | Invented term replaced with plain comparison. |
| What does not | What does not exist | Fragment completed. |
| The Open Conduct Alliance is a proposed institution, not an organization operating today. | The Open Conduct Alliance does not exist yet. | Thirteen words of hedging became six words of fact. |

---

## 12. Before publishing

1. Read every headline in order. Does the sequence describe the argument?
2. Any headline over fourteen words, or containing a metaphor?
3. Does every claim carry its status, and is every candidate visibly a candidate?
4. Does every legal, research, or precedent claim carry a source?
5. Any banned construction from §5?
6. Any limit softened, or any hypothesis hardened?
7. Does any page imply a partnership, an operating system, or completed review?
8. Could a hostile expert use this page to find the flaw? If not, what is missing?
9. If this page contradicts the versioned record, which one is wrong?
