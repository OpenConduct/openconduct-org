THE HUMAN-EXPERIENCE INPUT

Net Conduct Score

Net Conduct Score, or NCS, gives the human experience of an eligible interaction a structured place in a system that would otherwise record only transactions and business outcomes.

See the draft model ↓Challenge NCS →
CURRENT STATUS

NCS is a public proposal and research candidate. No certified implementation or official Net Conduct Score exists today. The normative requirements are in the NCS specification.

ONE BOUNDED QUESTION

The question is simple. Making its use fair is not.

Based only on how this customer interacted with you and others, would you want to serve them again?

An eligible worker answers only after enough direct interaction. Spending, loyalty, prior ratings, customer value, and internal risk labels are not shown.

Definitely yes+10
Probably yes+3
Probably not−3
Definitely not−10
Not enough interaction to sayOutside the score

The weights, aggregation, minimum evidence, recency, confidence, and public presentation remain research candidates rather than production settings.

WHAT IT MEASURES

One interaction, not a verdict on a person.

NCS represents an eligible direct participant's willingness to serve the customer again, based only on how that customer interacted with people during one verified interaction.

It can include patience, consideration, cooperation, directness, frustration, disagreement, or emotional intensity in context. It does not require cheerfulness, deference, small talk, cultural conformity, or making the work effortless.

WHAT IT EXCLUDES

NCS is not a shortcut to another score.

  • Spending, profitability, status, loyalty, or lifetime value
  • Complaint frequency, support use, or cost to serve
  • Compliance, obedience, agreeableness, or friendliness
  • Fraud, safety, credit, security, or identity risk
  • Manager opinion, automated inference, or internal notes
  • Employee performance or productivity
  • Public reputation, moral character, or universal rank
  • Tone, face, sentiment, demographics, or social media

ELIGIBILITY

A response counts only when the conditions are trustworthy.

  1. 01

    A real interaction is verified through an approved source.

  2. 02

    The worker directly participated and had enough exposure to answer.

  3. 03

    The role, response window, notice, and capture method match the approved context.

  4. 04

    The interaction was not selected to produce a favorable or unfavorable response.

  5. 05

    The worker can answer privately. Whether abstaining is safe in practice, especially in a small team, is unresolved.

  6. 06

    Prior CQ, loyalty, value, risk, fraud, and customer labels cannot influence the response.

RIGHTS ON BOTH SIDES

The worker gets privacy. The consumer gets review.

Worker protection

Answering is never mandatory, and no business may require a response or make one a condition of the role. No manager scripts, quotas, approval, performance use, retaliation, or individual-response access. Capture and any required training, review, or appeal participation are work and are compensated as such in every jurisdiction, not only where local law compels it.

Two limits remain unresolved. Whether declining feels free when an employer scheduled the task is a labor question rather than a design one. And a minimum number of eligible responders per location is required before capture can operate there at all, because in a very small team an abstention is itself identifying. That minimum does not exist yet.

Consumer protection

Consumers can correct source facts, question eligibility, report bias or selective sampling, add context, review the calculation, and escalate material harm. None of that requires learning the worker's identity or entering a personal dispute with them.

BEYOND CQ

NCS may have uses beyond customer relationships.

Agencies and clients, businesses and vendors, event participants, community hosts, and collaborators may find the question useful. None can inherit the consumer rules automatically.

Power, dependency, retaliation, collective activity, employment law, and access differ by context. Putting the NCS question into an employee review would not make that system aligned with Open Conduct.

HOW IT FITS

NCS supplies the human input. VCI supplies the record-based input. CQ combines them.

Net Conduct Score records the employee's experience. Verified Conduct Index uses approved business records to evaluate eligible responsibilities the customer accepted and could control. Neither input substitutes for the other.

When both inputs independently qualify, Conduct Quotient may combine them into one private score for a named context. Each input must pass its own gates first, so neither can substitute for the other. The synthesis formula then caps how far a strong input can lift a weak one.

WHERE IT COMES FROM

NCS is the Net Promoter Score question, pointed the other way.

Net Promoter Score asks a customer how they feel about a company. NCS asks a company's people how they feel about a customer. The shape is deliberately the same: one item, a small forced-choice set, and directional weights. Naming the lineage matters because it imports the criticism along with the format.

Single-item instruments carry known problems. Reliability cannot be estimated from one item the way it can from a scale. NPS in particular has been criticised for arbitrary category boundaries, for discarding information when responses are collapsed, and for producing differences that look meaningful and are not. Every one of those criticisms applies here and none is answered yet. The response weights, the cut points, and the decision to use a single item are all research candidates rather than settled choices.

RESEARCH AGENDA

Good research could produce a different NCS, or none at all.

  • Does the question measure willingness to serve again for the intended reason?
  • Do the four response labels communicate strength consistently across cultures, languages, roles, and contexts?
  • Does removing the midpoint clarify direction or force a judgment that is not there?
  • Is the abstention option understood and safe to use?
  • What history is sufficient before any summary becomes available?
  • Can workers respond without manager pressure, reidentification, or performance fear?
  • Can consumers understand, challenge, and recover from an incorrect or biased record?
